# 01 · Open the Box

Before preparations, before materials, before process — there is a box, and you open it. That is the first step, and it is physical. Everything in this manual is downstream of that single act.

## The Witness-Box

I call the art studio that houses the Art Operation **THE HISTORIOTHEQUE**, from the Greek ἵστωρ (*hístōr*, "witness") and θήκη (*thḗkē*, "box, chest"). Essentially, then: **the Historiotheque is a Witness-Box.**

Consider what a witness-box does. It holds things — and it testifies. A box that merely stores is a container; a box that testifies is a witness. Your studio must be both. It holds the work, the materials, the records — and it testifies that the work happened, when it happened, and by whose hand. Keep this double meaning in mind, because the entire documentation doctrine of this manual (Chapter 04) is already contained in the name: a witness that cannot testify is just furniture.

So when I say *Open The Box*, this is partly what I mean. You are opening a place whose job is to remember truthfully.

## Open up the shop

You have to open up the shop, first thing in the morning. Not when you feel like it. Not after the news, not after one more coffee — first thing. The shop does not open itself, and no one will open it for you. This is the operator's first decision of the day, and it is non-negotiable.

Opening the shop is not a metaphor for feeling inspired. It is a physical sequence: you enter the workspace, you survey its state (more on this in Chapter 08), you lay out what the day requires, and you begin. Some days the beginning is magnificent. Most days it is ordinary. The operation does not distinguish between the two — that is precisely what makes it an operation rather than a mood.

## In the Historiotheque

When you are in the studio working in your various 3D workspaces, or in your design laboratories, you are **IN THE HISTORIOTHEQUE**. Name it as such, out loud if necessary. The naming matters: a place with a name has a function, and a function has standards. The kitchen table where you also eat is not the Historiotheque. The corner of the studio where the operation lives — that is.

Designate it. Mark its boundaries. Everything inside the boundary is the operation; everything outside is the world. You will cross that boundary every day, in both directions, and the crossing is part of the discipline.

## You are a Historiotheque

*But* — and this is the deeper meaning of the Witness-Box — **you are also a Historiotheque.** When you are outside the studio, *In The Field*, collecting data, recording sound, photographing, noting, gathering the raw material of experience to rework *In The Lab*, you are a living, working, breathing, **mobile Historiotheque**.

This is the point the 2023 draft kept postponing, so let me state it plainly now: the operation is not the room. The room is where the operation sleeps. The operation itself is the operator's discipline, and the discipline is portable. A witness-box that only testifies indoors is a poor witness. Out in the field — on the soundwalk, on the dérive, in the grocery lineup where the idea strikes — you are still the box, still holding, still testifying. The notebook in your pocket is a Historiotheque. The phone recording in your coat is a Historiotheque. *You* are the Historiotheque, and the studio is where you unload.

## The operator

You open the box. You run the operation. Let me be explicit about what that makes you: **the operator** — not the audience, not the critic, not the brand, and not the prompt engineer.

A word on instruments, since it can no longer be avoided: artificial intelligence systems, software agents, assistants like the one helping me write this sentence — these are instruments in the shop, like brushes. A brush does not open the shop. No instrument, however sophisticated, is the operator; the operator is the one who decides what the operation is *for*, who opens the box in the morning, and who answers for what comes out of it. Use every instrument the century offers you. But never confuse the instrument with the hand that holds it, and never hand the hand's responsibility to the instrument. (The full theory of this is in Appendix A; the practice of it is every chapter that follows.)

## Read the manual

This manual you are holding is the *Instruction Manual* the box contains. Read it diligently, carefully, with attention to detail. **When you are lost, go back to the documentation** — this manual's, and your own. Implementation is where the production process begins — but you cannot start without reading the documentation. That sentence is not bureaucracy; it is mercy. Almost every operational failure I have witnessed in thirty years was someone implementing what they had not read.

## What to do today

1. **Name your box.** Designate the place where the operation lives — a room, a corner, a table that is yours. Say its name: the Historiotheque. Mark its boundary.
2. **Open it tomorrow morning, first thing.** Enter, survey the state of the workspace, lay out what the day requires, and begin. Ordinary is fine. Ordinary is the point.
3. **Take the stance.** Stand in the box and understand: no one else opens it. You are the operator. The instruments do not operate themselves.
4. **Walk out into the field and notice.** The box goes with you. The notebook, the recorder, the attention itself — you are the mobile Historiotheque now. Collect something and bring it back to the lab.
5. **Read Chapter 02.** You cannot prepare without knowing what preparation is. The road trip starts tomorrow.

The box is open. Now prepare.
