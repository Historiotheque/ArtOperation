# Appendix B · The Historiotheque: A Worked Example

*The manual describes an operation in the abstract. This appendix describes one in the concrete: my own. Every chapter above is running, right now, in the Historiotheque — the art studio, sound-design laboratory, and research operation I have run for thirty years. What follows is the proof that the manual works, chapter by chapter, because it is the manual's source.*

## The house and the field

The stationary Historiotheque is a one-bedroom apartment in the Vieux Village of Mont-Saint-Hilaire, Quebec — the art studio, the sound-design lab, and the research office in a single set of rooms. The mobile Historiotheque is everything else: the village streets for the soundwalk, the mountain trails for the dérive, the library for the refmats, the pocket notebook and the phone recorder that never leave my coat. One operation, several addresses (Chapter 08) — the studio manufactures, the field collects, the archive remembers, the public repositories testify.

## 01 · Open the Box, here

The shop opens first thing in the morning. The boundary is literal: the corner of the apartment where the operation lives is the Historiotheque; the rest is a home. Entering it, surveying it (Chapter 08's morning survey), laying out the day's work — that sequence has opened thousands of mornings. And the field kit is always packed, because the witness-box does not stop testifying outdoors: the soundwalk that became a field recording, the hike that became a series of photographs, the overheard sentence that became a refcard — all collected *as* the mobile Historiotheque, all unloaded In The Lab the same evening.

## 02 · Preparations, here

The six preparations are standing procedures, not aspirations:

1. **Workspace** — the studio corner, surveyable at a glance; the digital workspace under the same discipline (one canonical archive tree, mirrored to cloud and drives per the archive spec).
2. **Self** — the day's technique chosen deliberately; the Switchboard Method (see Chapter 03) deciding *which* project gets the next interval.
3. **Raw materials** — inventoried per discipline: paints and supports for the analog series, disk space and software for the digital, instruments for the sound work. Suppliers known; consumption rates known.
4. **Reference materials** — the Refcards-System, some fifteen years deep: one concept per card, numbered RC-YYYY-NNNN, cross-referenced across the whole operation. The archive beside it, consulted before any new work begins.
5. **Archives consulted** — the operation's memory is thirty years long; no series starts without asking what the archive already knows about its question.
6. **Publication prepared** — every work is born with its destination decided: filename conventions fixed in advance, the GitHub repository waiting, the Zenodo DOI pipeline ready. Nothing sits on a hard drive for six years anymore. That failure happened once, decades ago; the preparation exists because of it.

## 03 · Process, here

The operation runs in **series**, each with a definition and a collection target: the CHRONOTOPIUM-SERIES (mixed-media paintings, now at no. 11), the NOISE FIELDS (digital paintings as modulated noise), ANATOMY OF AN AESTHETIC PROBLEM (portraits as music-history research). Technique locked per series; populations generated, not masterpieces (dozens of noise-field variants per study); measurement against the real world at every iteration.

The Field/Lab cycle is the engine's two strokes: out for the soundwalk or the dérive with the recorder running, back to the lab where FL Studio, the paint table, or the Python environment manufactures the artifacts. Sessions are narrated — lifelogged — as they happen; the narration is the thinking, captured.

The daily rhythm is the **Switchboard Method**: the operator alternates across projects in short intervals, carrying no plan between them — only the next move — with logging and archiving automatic. The procedure runs the day; the operator runs the procedure.

## 04 · Documentation, here

The audit trail is public and inspectable: the GitHub repositories (the operation root, the artwork catalog, this manual, the Verifiability Stack), the Zenodo records with their DOIs, the ORCID profile linking them. The Declarations of Production-Year — written each fall for the year beginning the next October — are the operation's standing testimony: what was done, what continues, what was dropped and why.

The civic virtues are not aspirational here; they are the reason the repositories are public. Integrity: the log says what happened. Transparency: the procedure is visible. Truth: the record is corrected, never rewritten. Anyone can check — and the checking is the point. (The Verifiability Stack, Chapter 04's cross-reference, is itself a product of this operation: the documentation apparatus, documented.)

## 05 · Implementation, here

The Master Plan exists as the Declarations: the 2027–2028 declaration written in June 2025; the 2028–2029 declaration written in spring 2026, a full year early; the IMPORTANT UPDATE mechanism for amending the plan in writing, between series, never mid-run. Errors are logged as data — the archive contains the failed series and the dead ends alongside the successes, struck through and annotated, never deleted. Thirty years of implementation, diligently, is what "the operation compounds" looks like from the inside.

## 06 · Continuity, here

The chain is thirty years unbroken. That is not a boast; it is the operation's single most valuable asset, and everything in Chapter 06 exists to protect it. Savepoints are literal: sessions end with the next step written down, materials staged, the log closed, the studio surveyed. Minimum sessions exist for the worst days — the log has entries from days when the entry itself was the whole session, and those entries are among the most important in the record, because they are where the chain held thinnest and held.

## 07 · Practice, here

Rehearsal is scheduled: studies that will never be shown, exercises in unfamiliar media, the soundwalk with no recording obligation. Experiments run under protocol — bounded, hypothesized, logged — in designated space, never inside a production series. And learning never stopped: fourteen years into Python, now relearning it seriously *with* LLMs and agentic AI to build the cultural software the operation always needed; the reading list runs from thermodynamics to grief theory. The operator is still a student. That is the job.

## 08 · Workspace, here

The apartment studio is mapped, zoned, and surveyed every morning: the paint station, the digital station, the archive shelves, the field kit by the door. The Historiotopia is read like an instrument — the stack that grew overnight means a series is advancing; the tool left out means yesterday ended mid-thought. And the operation's other addresses — the field, the archive, the public repositories — get the same treatment: each surveyed, each ordered, each resumable. The logistics of Appendix A are not theory here; they are Tuesday.

## 09 · Edification, here

Run the examen honestly, and the operation grades itself:

- **Efficient** — yes, by design: the Switchboard eliminates decision overhead, the procedures eliminate reinvention.
- **Continuous** — thirty years, unbroken. The strongest item on the list.
- **Documented** — the audit trail is public and complete; the weakest gaps are the pre-digital years, being migrated as touched.
- **Redundant** — multiple copies, multiple venues; archival collapse engineered against.
- **Public** — the repositories, the DOIs, the declarations. It works in the light.
- **Honest about money** — the operation has never optimized for income and never promised it, including to itself.
- **Serving the work** — the ongoing discipline; the ego audited regularly, in writing.

Six held firmly, one (the pre-digital migration) in progress. That is what a mature Art Operation really looks like: not a triumph, but a checklist mostly checked, with the unchecked item named and scheduled. No mystique. Just the operation, continuing.
